# Topic 1 · Percentage, profit and loss

**Read first:** Basic book, the _Percentage_ chapter, then the _Profit and Loss_ chapter. Advanced book: the same two chapters, only after the practice set.

| Paper                                    | Question                                                                                                                                   | Answer  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| BB Assistant Programmer, 03.02.2023      | A team won 15 games and lost 9. These games are 16⅔% of the season. How many more games must it win to win 75% of the season?              | 93      |
| BB Asst Maintenance Engineer, 04.02.2023 | The profit when an item is sold for Tk 1920 equals the loss when it is sold for Tk 1280. At what price should it be sold for a 25% profit? | Tk 2000 |
| BUET non-dept MCQ, 19.06.2026 (Q72)      | Sold for Tk 150 at a 25% loss. Cost price?                                                                                                 | Tk 200  |

### Picture it

Think of **percent as a 100-taka note**. "20%" means "20 taka out of every 100 taka". Every percent question asks: _100 of what?_ That "what" is the **base**.

In a shop, the base is always the **cost price (CP)**: the money the shopkeeper paid. He buys at CP (his 100 taka). He sells at the selling price (SP). If SP is more, the extra is profit. If SP is less, the gap is loss. Profit % and loss % are always measured against what he paid, never against what he sold for.

A **marked price (MP)** is the price tag. A discount is cut from the tag, so the base for a discount is the MP.

### Understand it step by step

1. **x% of y** = x × y ÷ 100. Example: 15% of 640 = 96.
2. **What percent is a of b?** = a ÷ b × 100. The number after "of" is the base.
3. **Percent change** = change ÷ original × 100. Price 80 → 100: rise = 20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.
4. **"A is r% more than B" → B is less than A by** r ÷ (100 + r) × 100 %. A 25% more → B is 25 ÷ 125 × 100 = 20% less. The base changed, so the percent changed.
5. **Price up r%, keep spending the same → cut use by** r ÷ (100 + r) × 100 %. (Same formula; spending = price × quantity.)
6. **Two changes one after another (a% then b%)** = a + b + ab ÷ 100. Use minus signs for falls. +10% then −10% = 10 − 10 − 1 = **−1%** (a 1% fall, not zero).
7. **Profit = SP − CP; Loss = CP − SP.** Profit % = profit ÷ CP × 100.
8. **SP from CP:** SP = CP × (100 + profit%) ÷ 100, or CP × (100 − loss%) ÷ 100.
9. **CP from SP:** CP = SP × 100 ÷ (100 + profit%), or SP × 100 ÷ (100 − loss%).
10. **Marked price and discount:** SP = MP × (100 − discount%) ÷ 100. If MP is m% above CP and the discount is d%: profit % = (100 + m)(100 − d) ÷ 100 − 100.
11. **Two items sold at the same SP, one at x% profit, one at x% loss** → always a net **loss** of x² ÷ 100 %.
12. **Profit at S₁ equals loss at S₂** → CP = (S₁ + S₂) ÷ 2 (CP sits exactly in the middle).
13. **False weight:** a seller gives G grams for every 1000 g and charges for 1000 g. Gain % = (1000 − G) ÷ G × 100.

| Quantity              | Base (the "100") |
| --------------------- | ---------------- |
| Profit %, loss %      | Cost price       |
| Discount %            | Marked price     |
| Percent change        | Original value   |
| "A is r% more than B" | B                |

### Part 1 · Worked past question: BB 2023, profit equals loss

**Given:** profit at Tk 1920 = loss at Tk 1280.

1. Let CP = x. Profit = 1920 − x. Loss = x − 1280.
2. 1920 − x = x − 1280 → 2x = 3200 → **x = 1600**.
3. For 25% profit: SP = 1600 × 125 ÷ 100 = **Tk 2000**.

Shortcut check: CP = (1920 + 1280) ÷ 2 = 1600. ✔

### Part 2 · Worked past question: BB 2023, team wins

**Given:** won 15, lost 9 → 24 games played = 16⅔% of the season.

1. 16⅔% = 1/6. So the season = 24 × 6 = **144 games**.
2. 75% of 144 = **108 wins** needed.
3. Already won 15 → more wins needed = 108 − 15 = **93** (out of the 120 games left).

Trap: 16⅔% = 1/6, 33⅓% = 1/3, 12½% = 1/8, 6¼% = 1/16. Know these fractions; they turn hard percent into easy division.

### Part 3 · Worked past question: BUET 2026, loss

SP = 150 at 25% loss → CP = 150 × 100 ÷ 75 = **Tk 200**.

Trap: 150 + 25% of 150 = 187.5 is **wrong**. The loss is 25% of CP, not of SP.

### Mindmap

```mindmap
Percentage, profit & loss
  **Base first**
    profit/loss → CP
    discount → MP
    change → original value
  **Percent rules**
    x% of y = xy/100
    change/original × 100
    A r% more → B r/(100+r) less
    successive: a + b + ab/100
  **CP ↔ SP**
    SP = CP(100 ± p)/100
    CP = SP × 100/(100 ± p)
  **Shortcuts**
    profit at S₁ = loss at S₂ → CP = (S₁+S₂)/2
    same SP, ±x% → loss x²/100 %
    false weight: (1000−G)/G × 100
  **Marked price**
    SP = MP(100 − d)/100
    profit = (100+m)(100−d)/100 − 100
  **Fractions**
    16⅔ = 1/6, 33⅓ = 1/3
    12½ = 1/8, 6¼ = 1/16
```

### Remember it

- **"Ask: 100 of what?"** Find the base before any number.
- **"Profit lives on cost; discount lives on the tag."**
- **"Up and down by the same % is never zero"**: +x then −x is a loss of x²/100 %.
- **"Equal profit and loss? Cost is in the middle."**

### Exam trap

- Taking profit % on SP. Always divide by **CP** unless the question says "on selling price".
- Adding successive percents: 20% then 10% discount is **28%**, not 30%.
- Writing "loss = 0" for the same-SP ±x% case. It is always a small loss.
- Dropping the unit. Write "Tk 2000", not "2000".

### Exam answer · BB 2023 profit-loss question (5 marks)

Let the cost price be Tk x.

Profit when sold at Tk 1920 = 1920 − x.
Loss when sold at Tk 1280 = x − 1280.

Given that the profit equals the loss:
1920 − x = x − 1280
2x = 3200
x = 1600

So the cost price is Tk 1600.

Selling price for a 25% profit = 1600 × (100 + 25) ÷ 100 = 1600 × 125 ÷ 100 = Tk 2000.

**Answer: the item must be sold for Tk 2000.**

### Part 4 · Practice set 1 (closed book, 15 min)

1. The price of rice rises by 25%. By what percent must a family cut its rice use so that its spending stays the same?
2. An article is sold at a 10% loss. If it had been sold for Tk 90 more, there would have been a 5% profit. Find the cost price.
3. Two phones are sold for Tk 990 each. One gives a 10% profit and the other a 10% loss. Find the net profit or loss in taka and as a percent.
4. A shopkeeper marks goods 40% above cost and then gives a 15% discount. Find his profit percent.
5. A dishonest trader sells at cost price but uses a 900 g weight for 1 kg. Find his gain percent.

#### Solution

1. Cut = 25 ÷ (100 + 25) × 100 = **20%**.
2. From −10% to +5% is a gap of 15% of CP. 15% of CP = 90 → CP = 90 × 100 ÷ 15 = **Tk 600**.
3. CP₁ = 990 × 100 ÷ 110 = 900. CP₂ = 990 × 100 ÷ 90 = 1100. Total CP = 2000, total SP = 1980. **Loss Tk 20 = 1%** (check: 10² ÷ 100 = 1%).
4. SP = 140 × 85 ÷ 100 = 119 for every 100 of CP → **19% profit**.
5. Gain = (1000 − 900) ÷ 900 × 100 = **11⅑% (≈ 11.11%)**.

Scoring: 1 mark each. Steps must be written; a bare number gets ½.
